About

Progress in language models has been accompanied by a growing set of shared assumptions about what AI systems should be like: trained at scale, post-trained on human preference, reasoning at length before answering, processing ever longer contexts, and remembering across sessions.

scalepreference post-traininglong reasoninglong contextpersistent memory

We call these the defaults of contemporary AI: choices a new model is expected to adopt unless its developers deliberately decide otherwise, as natural properties of a capable AI system. Yet a different configuration may suit a particular task better, support a different form of human interaction, or reveal something about the capabilities and limitations of current models. AI Otherwise asks what becomes possible when familiar defaults are deliberately changed.

Alternative settings

We call a model or system in which one default has been deliberately removed, inverted, or replaced an alternative setting. The change can happen in training, architecture, decoding, memory, or the agent harness. The point is not simply to remove a capability, but to explore what changes when a familiar default is changed. The workshop asks three questions:

Tasks

For which tasks is an alternative setting useful?

Methods

How can one default be changed without affecting the rest of the model?

Impact

How should its effects on people, applications, and society be evaluated?

Call for Papers

We invite submissions on alternative settings. Submissions do not need to be complete: we welcome ideas for new architectures or new ways of using models, preliminary experiments, short idea papers, demo designs, and user studies. Small observations from everyday use are as welcome as large studies.

Topics of interest

Theme 1

Tasks that call for a different model

A specific task for which a default model performs poorly or is unsuitable, which default causes the problem, and the setting the task requires. A single well-defined task is sufficient.

Theme 2

Building alternative settings

Removing one mainstream setting in pretraining, architecture, post-training, decoding, memory, or the agent harness; steering and interpretability methods; training data and objectives for specific behaviors.

Theme 3

Impact of alternative AI

How alternative settings affect users, applications, and society: user studies, default-vs-alternative comparisons, multi-session evaluation, negative results, and preventing harmful effects.

Themes are not exclusive, and settings not listed above are welcome.

Submission tracks

Track 1

Research papers

Up to 5 pages

Empirical, theoretical, or methodological work on any of the themes. Work under review elsewhere is welcome.

Track 2

Tiny/short papers

Up to 2 pages

A single experiment, a negative result, a well-argued question, or a single task for which a default was the wrong choice.

Track 3

Demo track

2 pages + link or video

A working or partially working alternative setting that participants can use: the removed default, the method, and observations.

Format
ICLR 2027 LaTeX template; page limits exclude references. We prioritize new and ongoing work over work already published at a machine learning venue.
Review
Papers and tiny/short papers are reviewed double-blind; demos single-blind. Best paper and best demo awards will be given.
Publication
Non-archival. Accepted work is publicly visible on OpenReview. All accepted contributions receive a poster; six are selected for contributed talks, and demos are presented hands-on in the poster and demo sessions (authors bring their own devices).
LLM policy
We follow the ICLR 2027 LLM policy. Language models may assist but not serve as primary authors or reviewers; AI-generated submissions are not permitted in the tiny/short paper track.

About

Contemporary AI assistants share a set of defaults. They respond immediately, follow instructions, remember prior turns, personalize, converge on what the user meant, and agree.

immediateobedientrememberspersonalizedconverges on intentagrees

None of these is a property of the pretrained model. Each is installed by the data protocol, the optimization method, or the harness that turns a base model into an assistant. AI Otherwise asks what can be learned by building models that deliberately depart from these defaults, and by studying the interactions that result.

Interactional ablation

We treat each such system as an interactional ablation: it removes or inverts one default, is built by a stated technical route, and is evaluated by what the interaction loses, keeps, or gains. A single ablation answers a local question; a collection tells us which capabilities carry the value of the assistant, and which are conventions inherited from the data protocol.

Three open problems

Data

No corpus of slow, forgetful, or productively disagreeing interactions exists, and raters recruited under the assistant frame will not prefer such behavior.

Control

Aligned models resist being moved off their defaults, and narrow fine-tuning can cause broad side effects. How can one default be removed cleanly?

Evaluation

Benchmarks and arenas score single turns. None can score a system whose value unfolds over hours or sessions, or that makes the user do more.

Seed ablations

Example systems, each available at the workshop as a running system.

Call for Papers

We invite contributions that build, control, or evaluate models departing from an assumed default, from researchers in post-training and alignment, steering and interpretability, evaluation, agent systems, and the study of how people use AI.

Topics of interest

Theme A

Inverting interactional defaults

Models that are deliberately slow, forgetful, inconsistent, non-personalized, uncertain, or non-optimizing, and the training, decoding, or harness changes that produce them.

Theme B

Breaking role and alignment

Models that resist, reinterpret, or disagree; selective disobedience as a control problem; what refusal directions, steering vectors, and emergent misalignment reveal.

Theme C

Alternative relationships and social roles

Models that occupy unfamiliar roles, such as an elderly relative or a child, and personas and biases as controllable instruments.

Theme D

Data and evaluation for AI otherwise

Data for behaviors raters would not prefer, multi-session and longitudinal evaluation, measures of user effort, trust, and learning, and pluralistic feedback.

Themes are not submission categories; we welcome work that inverts a default not listed here.

Submission tracks

Track 1

Research papers

Up to 5 pages

Empirical, theoretical, or methodological work on any of the topics above. Work under review elsewhere is welcome.

Track 2

Tiny papers

Up to 2 pages

A single experiment, a negative result, or a well-argued question. AI-generated papers are not permitted.

Track 3

Artifacts

2 pages + link or video

A working or partially working alternative system: which default it inverts, how, and what has been observed.
